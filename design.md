# DeepSeekBot homepage

The homepage introduces DeepSeekBot to first-time visitors. Its visual authority is the
existing pixel town: pixel headings, wooden frames, system-font body copy, and the shared
day/night palette in `src/styles.css`.

## FAQ contract

- Place seven localized questions after Community and before the footer.
- Lead with the independent open-source project’s relationship to DeepSeek. Show this
  answer immediately; keep the remaining answers collapsed until requested.
- Use one wooden frame with divided rows. Native details/summary controls allow multiple
  answers to stay open and work with the keyboard.
- Keep question and answer text readable in the body font. A pixel plus/minus shows state;
  the complete question row is the interaction target.
- Explain capabilities, getting started, models, costs, data and permissions, and chat
  connections. Keep provider fees, inference data flow and release limitations explicit.
- Put relevant localized guide links beside their answers. The homepage retains its
  existing language alternates, sitemap entries and navigation.
- At narrow widths, wrap questions and guide links while retaining a visible toggle and
  focus outline. Both themes must use the shared palette and avoid horizontal overflow.

Success means the affiliation answer is visible by default, all seven questions can be
operated independently with pointer or keyboard, Chinese and English links resolve, and
desktop/mobile views remain readable in both themes.

## Blog index grid contract

- Readers scan article covers, dates, titles and summaries before choosing an article.
- Stretch each card's wooden frame to the height of its grid row, so cards with shorter
  summaries share the same bottom edge as their neighbors.
- Let the longest content determine each row's height. Keep full titles and summaries
  visible, with natural card heights when the grid becomes a single column.
- Preserve the existing cover ratio, spacing, typography, day/night palette and link focus.

Success means the Chinese and English grids align card bottoms within each row at desktop
and tablet widths, while mobile cards stay readable without clipping or horizontal overflow.
